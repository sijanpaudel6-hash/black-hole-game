# Making Black Hole: Event Horizon Fun — Researched Ideas

Nov 1, 2026 · @Sijan Paudel

## The answer

**The game is boring because the player never has to decide anything, nothing surprises them, and nothing in the universe reacts to them.** Adding levels or polish won't fix that. Change the structure in three ways, in this order:

1. **Turn levels into runs with choices ("One Life of a Black Hole").** Each run takes the black hole from the early universe to the far future. At every growth tier you pick 1 of 3 cosmic powers that combine in surprising ways, as in Vampire Survivors and Balatro. Every run plays differently.
2. **Make the universe talk back (AI).** Planets with civilisations send you messages as you approach: they beg, bargain, threaten, or offer a deal. You choose to eat them or spare them, and that choice matters. This is the personality the game lacks, and it is made for Shorts.
3. **Add Cosmic Craft.** Smash objects together to create new ones: two neutron stars make a kilonova that forges gold, as in real astrophysics. Known physics is hand-written; an AI invents the long tail, with "First Discovery" credit shared across all players, the way Infinite Craft went viral.

**To make it spread, build sharing into the game itself:** a Daily Universe with a Wordle-style share card, Feed Your Hometown ("comment your city and I'll feed it next"), and stats showing how many players spared each civilisation. See "Built to go viral" below.

All three use the space topics people search most: wormholes, Sagittarius A\*, quasars, supernovae, neutron stars and dark matter each get 100K–1M Google searches a month worldwide, per your Keyword Planner.

## Why it feels boring now

**Six things are missing, and each one is something successful games deliberately build in.**

| Missing | What it feels like now | What hit games do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decisions | Always eat the nearest smaller thing | Vampire Survivors and Balatro offer a choice of upgrades every few minutes, and combinations change the run |
| Surprise | Every level has the same objects and the same goal | Infinite Craft's unpredictable results were a large part of its appeal |
| Tension | Danger is a few rocks; failure is a timer | Vampire Survivors alternates easy stretches with tense swarms to keep players in flow |
| Personality | Objects are shapes; nothing reacts to you | Suck Up! and Whispers from the Star built whole games around characters who answer back |
| Discovery | Science lives in pop-up cards, not in play | Outer Wilds makes learning how its solar system works the gameplay itself |
| Sense of scale | Growth is a number going up | Katamari's designer worried players forget how small they started, and wished for a stronger "Wow, I've gotten so big" |

The fix is not more of the same content. Each idea in this doc targets one or more of these six gaps.

## Big new ideas

**Eight ideas, from structural changes to cheap spectacle.** Ideas 1–3 are the recommended core; 4–8 add flavour on top.

### 1. One Life of a Black Hole (runs instead of levels)

Each run is one black hole's life, 10–15 minutes from the early universe to the far future. Death ends the run; permanent unlocks carry over.

**The eras (situations):**

1. **Cosmic Dawn** — you are a tiny primordial black hole in a fog of gas. Tiny black holes really do evaporate faster through Hawking radiation, so you shrink unless you keep eating. This replaces the timer with real physics.
2. **First Stars** — giant early stars burn out and go supernova around you: a feast, plus a shockwave to ride or escape.
3. **Galaxy Era** — named rival black holes compete for the same food. Outgrow them to merge, or flee.
4. **Quasar Era** — food is everywhere; feed fast enough and you ignite as a quasar.
5. **Far Future** — stars are dying and food runs out. The run ends with you as the last light in the universe, slowly evaporating. A haunting, very shareable finale.

**Pick 1 of 3 cosmic powers at every era change.** Powers combine:

| Power | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Kerr Spin | Your pull becomes a swirl; objects orbit before falling in |
| Relativistic Jets | Tap to fire twin jets that smash big objects into edible chunks |
| Tidal Shredder | Objects slightly bigger than you crumble when you pass close |
| Dark Matter Halo | An invisible halo widens your pull |
| Gravitational Lens | Time slows near massive objects; hidden objects appear |
| Hawking Bloom | Losing mass releases a burst that pushes hazards away |

Synergies (the Balatro moment): **Spin + Jets = Pulsar Lighthouse** (jets sweep in a circle), **Spin + Shredder = Accretion Buzzsaw**, **Jets + Lens = jets that curve around stars**.

*Fixes:* decisions, surprise, tension.

### 2. The Universe Talks Back (AI civilisations)

Some planets host civilisations, from stone-age tribes to star-spanning empires. As you approach, they broadcast a short message to you, generated by AI from the situation (their technology level, personality, your size, what you just ate):

- A tribe holds a ritual to calm the "hungry star in the sky".
- A scientific civilisation asks you to wait while they finish uploading their history to a probe.
- An empire threatens you, then fires missiles that nudge you off course.
- A desperate planet offers a deal: spare us and we'll show you where a neutron star is hiding.

You choose: **Eat** (mass, and nearby civilisations hear about it and arm themselves or flee) or **Spare** (they become allies, give a boon or reveal a secret). A **Galactic News** ticker reports your deeds in AI-written headlines.

*Fixes:* personality, decisions. *Why it works:* every encounter is a moral choice with a punchline, the kind of clip people share ("aliens begged me to spare them").

### 3. Cosmic Craft (collide things to make new things)

In Sandbox, fling objects into each other to create new ones, Infinite Craft style:

- **Real science (hand-written, marked REAL):** neutron star + neutron star → kilonova, which forges gold; two white dwarfs → Type Ia supernova; black hole + star → tidal disruption flare; two galaxies → elliptical galaxy; black hole + black hole → a bigger black hole and gravitational waves.
- **Imagined (AI-generated, marked IMAGINED):** any combination nobody has tried gets a name, an icon and a one-line description, saved for every player. The first finder gets "First Discovery" credit.

*Fixes:* discovery, surprise. *Why it works:* it turns your science content into play, and "I made gold by crashing neutron stars" is a perfect Short.

### 4. Real cosmic events (cheap spectacle)

Random events from real astrophysics, each a 10–30 second situation:

- **Supernova:** a star collapses, then explodes into a cloud of debris to feast on.
- **Tidal disruption:** shred a star into a glowing stream and slurp it like spaghetti.
- **Wandering black hole:** a rogue black hole tears through at high speed, leaving new stars behind it, like RBH-1 from 2026.
- **Wormhole** (theoretical): a portal to a bonus zone.
- **White hole** (hypothetical): spits out matter, a hazard you must dodge.
- **Dark matter:** invisible food you spot only by how it bends starlight.
- **Gravitational wave chirp:** every merger ripples the screen with a rising "chirp" sound.

### 5. Mission Control (AI scientist companion)

A scientist character watches you through a telescope and comments live: explaining what you just did ("that Moon was torn apart by tidal forces"), reacting to records, and teasing you when you crash. Mostly pre-written lines, with AI for rare moments. It also narrates your Shorts automatically.

### 6. Named rivals with grudges

Hole.io's tension, without servers: rival black holes have names, personalities and memories ("Hungry Hugo still remembers you stole his star"). They taunt you, hold grudges across runs, and get their revenge. Add a daily seeded race against other players' recorded ghosts.

### 7. Your run becomes a song

Built on NASA-style sonification: every object you eat adds a note or texture to your black hole's hum. By the end, each run has its own eerie soundtrack you can export with the clip.

### 8. Fall In (a 2-minute experience)

Answering "what happens if you fall into a black hole", one of the fastest-rising searches: you are an astronaut falling in. Light bends around you, the outside universe squeezes into a bright circle overhead, then spaghettification. Short, educational, and made for a viral clip.

## Built to go viral

**Games spread when playing them creates something people want to post: a result to compare, a choice to argue about, or a moment that's personal or funny.** Clips alone aren't enough; the sharing has to be part of the game.

What the research shows:

- **Wordle** went from about 90 players to over 2 million in roughly two months. One puzzle a day for everyone made results comparable, and the spoiler-free emoji grid packed the whole drama of a game into one tiny post ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/heres-why-the-word-game-wordle-went-viral-180979439/)).
- **Suika Game** spread through streamers: simple rules, physics chaos where one bump can end the game, satisfying chain reactions, and a score challenge everyone chased. It passed 10 million downloads ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suika_Game)).
- **A solo dev's TikTok** reached about 1M views overnight and quadrupled his wishlists. Asking viewers for ideas produced hundreds of comments, which fed the algorithm ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/from-400-to-20-000-wishlists-with-1-video-how-tiktok-can-make-your-game-go-viral)).
- **Telltale's Walking Dead** showed what percentage of players made each hard choice, which got people comparing themselves and talking ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-walking-dead-episode-3-stats-show-most-of-us-still-5949251)).
- **Infinite Craft** went viral on the surprise of funny, unpredictable results people screenshot.

### The viral features, ranked

**1. Daily Universe + share card (the Wordle move).** Every day, everyone gets the same universe seed for one run. At the end you get a share card: a strip of emoji telling your black hole's life (🌑 → 🪨 → 🌍 → ⭐ → 🌌 → 💀), with lines like "Ate 3 civilisations, spared 1, became a quasar, lived 13.8 billion years · Universe #312" and a link. A text version for WhatsApp and X, an image version for Instagram and TikTok. *Fun:* a daily reason to play and a fair way to compare with friends. *Cost:* low; the daily seed comes from the date, no server needed.

**2. Feed Your Hometown.** Type any city or country and your black hole approaches Earth, zooms in, and swallows it, label and all: "KATHMANDU · SWALLOWED". Cities and countries only, never addresses, so nobody can target a person. *Viral loop:* the Short ends with "Comment your city and I'll feed it next", which is the comment-driven trick from the TikTok case, built into the game. Every comment is your next video. *Cost:* low to medium; ship a list of world cities with coordinates.

**3. "Only 14% of players spared them."** After every eat-or-spare decision with a civilisation, show what share of players made the same choice, like Telltale's stats. People screenshot the moment they were in the minority and argue in the comments. *Cost:* medium; needs a tiny counter on a server. Start with the decision screens, add live percentages later.

**4. Screenshot-able AI moments.** Galactic News headlines about your deeds ("BREAKING: Local black hole eats Saturn, claims it was 'mostly rings'"), civilisations' last words, and Cosmic Craft's weirdest discoveries. Pre-generated, so funny, safe and free. *Cost:* low.

**5. One famous challenge number.** Suika had "reach 3,000". Give this game one: "Eat the Milky Way in under 10 minutes", with a global leaderboard and the time shown on the share card. Streamers love a clear target. *Cost:* low for the challenge; a leaderboard needs a small server.

**6. An ending that makes people feel something.** The Far Future finale (you, the last light in a dying universe, slowly evaporating) is the kind of quiet, emotional moment people clip and react to. *Cost:* part of idea 1.

## Using AI in the game

**Use AI where it *is* the gameplay (civilisations, Cosmic Craft), generate as much as possible ahead of time, and cache everything else.** That keeps it cheap, fast and safe for a free browser game.

**Lessons from AI games so far** ([Wanderfolk overview](https://wanderfolk-website.pages.dev/ai-npcs-in-games), written by an AI-game studio, so read it with that in mind):

- AI works when it is the core mechanic and is tied to game systems (reputation, consequences), not when it only adds chat.
- Cloud AI adds roughly 1–3 seconds of delay per reply, which hurts real-time games.
- Each conversation is an API call, so costs grow with players; this is hardest for free browser games.
- AI characters can break character or say odd things; strong personalities, game state in the prompt and filters reduce this.
- A December 2025 Quantic Foundry survey found 85% of gamers view generative AI in games below neutral. Players object to AI as a shortcut replacing artists and writers, not to AI as gameplay.

**How each AI feature should work:**

| Feature | Approach | Runtime cost | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Civilisation messages | **Pre-generate** thousands of messages offline (species × tech level × mood × situation) and ship them in the game | None | Repetition after many runs; add new batches with updates |
| Galactic News headlines | Pre-generated templates with slots for your deeds | None | Low |
| Cosmic Craft (imagined results) | Infinite Craft model: AI generates a result the first time a pair is combined, saves it, every player gets the same answer | Only for brand-new combinations; falls as the database fills | Odd or unsafe results: content filter + human review queue |
| Mission Control rare comments | Mostly pre-written; live AI only for rare moments, requested a few seconds early to hide the delay | Low | Delay; character drift |
| Talking back to civilisations (later) | Live AI with free text or voice, Whispers-style | Highest | Cost, safety with young players, delay |

**Technical must-haves:**

- The AI key can never be in the game's code (anyone could copy it). Live features need a small server function in between that holds the key, rate-limits players and caches answers.
- Use a small, fast model (for example Claude Haiku) with strict instructions: stay in character, family-friendly, under 25 words.
- Every AI feature needs a non-AI fallback, so the game still works offline, on a portal that blocks outside requests, or if the server is down. Check each portal's rules on external network calls before launch.
- Keep the art and music human-made or from free libraries, and present AI as "a living universe", not as a cost cut.

## What to build next

**Six sessions turn the game from "eat things" into "live a black hole's life". Playtest after Session B before going further.** None of A–F needs a paid AI service: Claude Code can write the civilisation messages itself during the session and ship them as a data file.

- [ ] **Session A – Runs and eras.** Replace fixed levels with a run through five eras (Cosmic Dawn → First Stars → Galaxy Era → Quasar Era → Far Future). Hawking evaporation replaces the timer in Cosmic Dawn. Keep current levels as a "Classic" mode.
- [ ] **Session B – Cosmic powers.** Pick 1 of 3 at each era change; six powers and three synergies from idea 1. **→ Playtest gate: do runs feel different from each other?**
- [ ] **Session C – The Universe Talks Back.** Civilisation planets with eat/spare choices, boons and consequences, plus the Galactic News ticker. Claude Code writes a bank of 300+ short messages into `src/data/civilisations.js`.
- [ ] **Session D – Go viral.** Daily Universe (same seed for everyone from the date), the end-of-run share card (text + image, using the phone's share menu), Feed Your Hometown with a bundled list of world cities, and the "Eat the Milky Way in under 10 minutes" challenge timer.
- [ ] **Session E – Cosmic events.** Supernova, tidal disruption, wandering black hole, wormhole, white hole, dark matter and the merger chirp, as random events in runs and as "What if…?" scenarios.
- [ ] **Session F – Cosmic Craft (real recipes).** Collisions in Sandbox with 30+ hand-written real-science recipes and REAL badges.
- [ ] **Later – Live features.** Choice percentages and a global leaderboard (a small server that counts), then AI-generated Cosmic Craft results, Mission Control's rare lines and talking back to civilisations. This needs a server and an AI account with an API key that you set up yourself.

Ready-to-paste prompt for Session A:

> Read SPEC.md, design/DESIGN.md and this ideas doc's "One Life of a Black Hole" section (pasted below). We are on Session A – Runs and eras. Replace the 15 fixed levels as the main mode with a single run through five eras: Cosmic Dawn, First Stars, Galaxy Era, Quasar Era, Far Future. In Cosmic Dawn the black hole slowly loses mass unless it eats (Hawking evaporation, tunable in config.js). Era changes use the existing tier-up moment. Keep the old levels as a Classic mode. Don't add powers yet. Make sure it runs with npm run dev, then commit.

Check your remaining cloud credits before starting; if they are low, do A, B, C and D first. A–C fix the boredom; D makes it spread.
